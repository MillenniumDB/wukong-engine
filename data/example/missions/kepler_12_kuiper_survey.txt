MERIDIAN DEEP SPACE AGENCY
MISSION REPORT — KEPLER-12 (INTERIM)

Mission code: KEPLER-12
Designation: Outer Kuiper Belt Survey
Status: active
Launch: 8 January 2191
Planned recovery: 2195
Vehicle: MDV Perihelion (AUR-01), Aurora-class survey vessel


1. OBJECTIVE

KEPLER-12 is a long-duration survey of the outer Kuiper belt, tasked with
cataloguing bodies above 40 km diameter in the 47-58 AU band and with
returning continuous particle-flux measurements along the cruise path. It is
the first operational flight of the Aurora-class survey vessel MDV Perihelion
(AUR-01) and the agency's longest crewed mission to date.

This is an interim report covering the first 26 months of flight. A final
report will follow recovery.


2. CREW

KEPLER-12 flies with a crew of three on a rotating watch schedule derived from
the habitability data returned by ARTEMIS-7:

- Cmdr. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233), mission commander. Promoted to command
  following his service as pilot on ARTEMIS-7.
- Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190), science officer. Responsible for the survey
  instrument suite, having previously served as science officer on ARTEMIS-7.
- Lt. Ravi Sundaram (MDSA-2408), flight engineer. Responsible for the
  propulsion and life-support systems of MDV Perihelion (AUR-01).


3. PROGRESS TO DATE

MDV Perihelion (AUR-01) crossed 47 AU on flight day 612 and began the primary
survey on schedule. As of this report, 1,184 bodies have been catalogued
against a mission target of 1,500, and the particle-flux record is complete
with the exception of the gap described in section 4.

Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) has reported that the survey instrument is
outperforming its specification at low phase angles, and has requested an
extension of the 58 AU boundary to 61 AU. The request is under review.

Lt. Ravi Sundaram (MDSA-2408) has carried out two unscheduled maintenance
cycles on the primary radiator loop, both successful.


4. ANOMALIES

Incident INC-0517 was recorded on flight day 703: a communications blackout of
31 hours caused by a fault in the high-gain antenna gimbal of MDV Perihelion
(AUR-01). Telemetry was recovered in full from onboard storage once the link
was restored, but the particle-flux record carries a 31-hour gap during which
the instrument was powered down as a precaution. Cmdr. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233)
elected to power down rather than run the instrument without a downlink, a
decision endorsed by the review board.

No other incidents have been recorded.


5. ASSESSMENT

The mission is within its planned envelope on consumables, propellant and
crew-hours. The Aurora-class survey vessel MDV Perihelion (AUR-01) has
performed to specification apart from the gimbal fault described above, which
has been traced to a supplier batch also fitted to the crew transport MDV
Cassiopeia (VSP-03) and is being tracked agency-wide.

Mission planning recommends that the 61 AU extension be approved conditional
on the outcome of the propellant review scheduled for the next reporting
period.


6. CONSUMABLES AND CREW HOURS

Propellant remaining is 61% of launch load against a planned 58% at this
point in the flight, the margin having accrued from the shallower-than-planned
insertion. Life-support consumables are at 64% with no off-nominal
consumption recorded. Both figures support the 61 AU extension without a
resupply, though the propellant review will be the deciding input.

Crew hours are tracking below the planned envelope. The rotating watch
schedule adopted from the ARTEMIS-7 habitability dataset has held up over 26
months, and Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) reports no measurable degradation in
task performance across the crew. Lt. Ravi Sundaram (MDSA-2408) has absorbed
the two unscheduled radiator cycles without exceeding his duty limits.


7. FORWARD PLAN

The next reporting period covers flight days 790 to 1,030 and contains three
scheduled activities.

First, completion of the primary survey to the 58 AU boundary. At the current
catalogue rate the mission target of 1,500 bodies will be met with margin, and
Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) expects to close the catalogue ahead of the
boundary.

Second, the propellant review. If the margin holds, mission planning will
recommend the 61 AU extension requested by Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) and MDV
Perihelion (AUR-01) will continue outbound rather than beginning the return
cruise.

Third, the high-gain antenna gimbal. The supplier batch review opened after
incident INC-0517 is expected to report within the period. Until it does,
Cmdr. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233) has standing authority to operate the antenna
under manual pointing at a reduced slew rate, and Lt. Ravi Sundaram
(MDSA-2408) carries out a pointing check before every downlink window.

No crew rotation is planned. MDV Perihelion (AUR-01) will remain under the
command of Cmdr. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233) through recovery.
