MERIDIAN DEEP SPACE AGENCY
MISSION REPORT — ARTEMIS-7

Mission code: ARTEMIS-7
Designation: Sun-Earth L2 Relay Deployment
Status: completed
Launch: 14 March 2189
Recovery: 2 September 2189
Vehicle: MDV Cassiopeia (VSP-03), Vesper-class crew transport


1. OBJECTIVE

ARTEMIS-7 was tasked with deploying the L2 Relay Array, a three-node
communications installation at the Sun-Earth L2 point, and with certifying the
crew transport MDV Cassiopeia (VSP-03) for extended crewed operations beyond
cislunar space. Secondary objectives covered long-duration habitability data
and a shakedown of the array's autonomous station-keeping software.

The mission was approved following the loss of the Tycho ground relay in 2187,
which left deep-space telemetry dependent on a single downlink path. ARTEMIS-7
was the agency's first crewed deployment beyond the lunar gateway in eleven
years.


2. CREW

The mission flew with a crew of three:

- Cmdr. Amara Okonkwo (MDSA-2071), mission commander. Responsible for overall
  mission authority, EVA sequencing and the L2 insertion burn.
- Lt. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233), pilot. Responsible for the flight of VSP-03,
  proximity operations during node release, and the recovery trajectory.
- Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190), science officer. Responsible for array
  calibration, the habitability instrument suite and the medical log.


3. FLIGHT SUMMARY

Launch occurred on 14 March 2189 with no holds. MDV Cassiopeia (VSP-03)
completed its trans-L2 injection on flight day 3 and arrived on station on
flight day 41. Lt. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233) flew all three proximity approaches
manually after the automated approach system produced an unacceptable closure
rate on the first attempt.

Node A and Node B were released and activated on flight days 44 and 46
respectively. Node C release was deferred by six days following the hull
anomaly described in section 4. Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) completed the full
calibration sequence for all three nodes by flight day 58, and the array was
handed over to ground control on flight day 61.

Cmdr. Amara Okonkwo (MDSA-2071) logged 22 hours 40 minutes of EVA across four
excursions, the longest cumulative EVA time recorded on a Vesper-class
transport.


4. ANOMALIES

Incident INC-0412 was recorded on flight day 45, when a micrometeoroid strike
produced a hull breach in the aft equipment bay of MDV Cassiopeia (VSP-03).
The bay was isolated within 90 seconds. Cmdr. Amara Okonkwo (MDSA-2071) and
Lt. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233) completed the patch during an unscheduled EVA on
flight day 47. No crew injury resulted, and the incident is treated in full in
the dedicated incident report.

A second, minor anomaly involving intermittent star-tracker dropouts was
logged but not assigned an incident code, as the behaviour was traced to a
known firmware defect already scheduled for correction.


5. OUTCOME

All primary objectives were met. The L2 Relay Array has been operational and
within specification since handover. MDV Cassiopeia (VSP-03) was certified for
extended crewed operations on return, with the standing recommendation that
aft bay shielding be uprated before any mission of comparable duration.

The habitability dataset gathered by Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) was released to
the agency's mission planning office and has since informed the crew rotation
schedule adopted for KEPLER-12.


6. TIMELINE

Flight day 1 — Launch from the equatorial range. No holds.
Flight day 3 — Trans-L2 injection burn executed by Lt. Theo Vance
  (MDSA-2233). Residuals within tolerance.
Flight day 41 — Arrival on station. L2 insertion burn executed by Cmdr. Amara
  Okonkwo (MDSA-2071).
Flight day 42 — First proximity approach aborted after the automated approach
  system commanded an unacceptable closure rate.
Flight day 44 — Node A released and activated. Approach flown manually.
Flight day 45 — Incident INC-0412. Pressure alarm in the aft equipment bay of
  MDV Cassiopeia (VSP-03); bay isolated.
Flight day 46 — Node B released and activated.
Flight day 47 — Unscheduled EVA. Hull patch applied by Cmdr. Amara Okonkwo
  (MDSA-2071) and Lt. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233).
Flight day 52 — Node C released and activated, six days behind plan.
Flight day 58 — Calibration of all three nodes completed by Dr. Mei Liang
  (MDSA-2190).
Flight day 61 — L2 Relay Array handed over to ground control.
Flight day 173 — Recovery.


7. RECOMMENDATIONS

R1. Uprate aft bay shielding on all Vesper-class transports to the
forward-bay standard before flying any mission exceeding 120 days. Raised in
response to incident INC-0412 and carried into the fleet review.

R2. Retain manual proximity operations as the primary approach mode for relay
node deployment until the automated approach system has been requalified. The
manual approaches flown by Lt. Theo Vance (MDSA-2233) were faster and used
less propellant than the automated attempt.

R3. Adopt the rotating watch schedule derived from the habitability dataset
returned by Dr. Mei Liang (MDSA-2190) for all missions beyond 120 days. This
recommendation has already been taken up by KEPLER-12.

R4. Stow no experiment racks in the aft equipment bay of a Vesper-class
transport until R1 is closed. Two racks were lost to vacuum exposure during
INC-0412.

R5. Schedule the star-tracker firmware correction before the next crewed
deployment beyond cislunar space. The dropouts observed on this flight did not
warrant an incident code but consumed crew attention during a critical phase.
